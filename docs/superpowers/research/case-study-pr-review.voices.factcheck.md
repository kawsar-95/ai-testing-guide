# Voice fact-check: From Issue to Reviewed PR (round 1, 2026-10-20)

Page: `content/case-study/pr-review.mdx`. I did not write the voices.

Method: I read the 9 `<Voices>` blocks. For each claim in a `<Layman>` or `<Bangla>` panel, I found the `<Technical>` sentence of the same `##` section that it restates. A claim that is not in the Technical panel, or that changes a number, a version, a flag, a command, a path, a product name, a scope, or a condition, is a FAIL. An omission is not a FAIL. For each `<Cite n>` in a voice panel, I checked that the Technical panel of the same section cites the same `n` for the same claim. I counted the numbers again (the 41 acceptance criteria, the 7 steps, the 3 files and +82/−22, the traceability rows). I read the Bangla text for meaning and natural use. I checked politeness (আপনি), English technical terms, and the hyphen between an English word and a Bangla suffix. The page has no `## Key words` section, and the `## Sources` area has no voices.

Notes from the check:

- The Layman and Bangla panels of "What the review found" and "The zero-width-space finding" keep the same numbers and verdicts as the Technical panel.
- Two words are firmer or wider than the Technical text, but they do not change a number, a scope, or a condition. They are language notes, not FAILs: "must reference" (row 36) and "each backend endpoint path" (row 107).
