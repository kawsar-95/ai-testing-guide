I started only my own `sleep NNN` processes (unique numbers 20, 30, 60–62, 301–315), signalled only their PIDs (from `$!`, or the PID of my own zombie child), and checked `/proc/PID/cmdline` before each signal. I did not touch any process that I did not start. No `pkill`, `killall`, `kill -1`, negative nice, `ulimit` change outside a subshell, fork bomb, or root command. All my processes were gone at the end (`pgrep -x sleep` showed none of mine). Block 3 (job control) ran in a pty with interactive Bash.

## The writer's weak points (answers)

- **Unsourced statements labeled as advice.** The `ps` of macOS or BusyBox (a definite claim) and the CI sentence in the Background tip ("Some CI systems end all processes...") and in the Signals tip ("CI systems cancel a job with a signal") state facts: FAIL, with a conditional rewording of the same length. "Other versions of `pgrep` may use these letters differently", the fork-bomb warning, and the `limits.conf` second-session advice are hedges or pure advice: OPINION-OK.
- **`pkill -f` wrapper sentence.** It states only the `-f` match rule (the cite to pgrep(1) supports that) and does not say the wrapper is killed: PASS. Reproduced with `pgrep -f` only: a `bash -c '... pgrep -a -f "sleep 305"'` wrapper lists itself; the same commands in a script file do not. Nothing was signalled except my own `sleep`.
- **`ionice`.** The sentence is careful ("its man page describes the CFQ I/O scheduler, so check that your kernel honors it"). On this kernel the schedulers are `mq-deadline` and `none`, and `ionice -p` still prints `idle`: the class is stored and may have no effect. PASS.
- **Roadmap overlap hit.** Confirmed: the hit is the URL of source 3, which also stands in line 71 of `119.txt`. Not a copied sentence. No FAIL.
- **Other facts that I tested and that were wrong or too wide:** a script with `kill %1` (works in Bash, fails in `sh`), SIGHUP at exit (plain `exit` does not kill background jobs; a closed terminal does), `nohup.out` (only when stdout is a terminal), `renice` as non-root (a nice resource limit allows lowering), "128 plus N" (not in the cited page), Ctrl+Z to SIGTSTP (not in the cited pages), and `killall`/`pkill` "no clean-up" (they send SIGTERM by default).
