## Tutorial's own text (no cite)

- Own computation, checked by a short script (same rules as S1 for the geohash, same formulas as S3 for the quadkey; the script reproduces `u17`, `u173zy3j`, `u173zvfz`, and `u173zt90` of S1): the point 52.374081 N, 4.912350 E (the first point of the S1 example) has geohash `u17` at length 3, `u173z` at length 5, and `u173zy3j` at length 8. Its quadkey is `1` at level 1, `12` at level 2, `120` at level 3, and `120202` at level 6. Each quadkey starts with the quadkey of the level before.
- Own computation: at 52.5 N, longitude 5.624 gives `u17` and longitude 5.626 gives `u1k` at length 3. The east edge of `u17` is 5.625 (S1 bbox). The two points are about 135 m apart (0.002 degree of longitude, about 111.32 km per degree times cos 52.5 degrees). They share only the first 2 characters.
- Own arithmetic: the `u17` box is 1.40625 degrees high and 1.40625 degrees wide (53.4375 - 52.03125, 5.625 - 4.21875). A level N has 4^N tiles (2^N by 2^N).
- Own reading: both a geohash (S2) and a quadkey (S3) interleave the bits of two coordinates into one key, and a longer key means a smaller cell.
- Own reading: the 1+8 areas of S2 are how a search handles a shape that reaches over the edge of one cell. The page says this is the tutorial's own link.
- The "nearby shops" example and the tester tips.
