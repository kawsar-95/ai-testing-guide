# Defects and the 7 Principles

Errors, defects, bugs, and failures, and the seven principles of testing.

<PageMeta />

## Error, Defect, Bug and Failure

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

- **Error**: Human beings make errors (mistakes), or misunderstanding requirements, which produce defects (faults). Humans make errors for various reasons, such as time pressure, complexity of work products, processes, infrastructure or interactions, or simply because they are tired or lack adequate training.
- **Defects**: By any types of error, it leads to defect. Defects can be found in documentation, such as a requirements specification or a test script, in source code, or in a supporting artifact such as a build file.
- **Failure**: Failure is the observable outcome of the bug when the software is executed in an operational environment, the system may fail to do what it should do, or do something it shouldn't, causing a failure. If the software can not perform as per business need, then it is considered as failure. In another way, if the end user is not satisfied or found issues in the software, that is the ultimate failure.

<Callout type="warning">

The notes tie a failure to the operational environment and to user satisfaction, but the ISTQB glossary defines a failure as an event where a component or system does not meet its requirements during execution, and the syllabus says that dynamic testing also triggers failures. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

- **Bug**: If a defect is caught by executing the test object and accepted by developer, then this is called bug. To prove the developers that it is a bug, needs bug advocacy.

<Callout type="warning">

The notes treat a bug as a different thing from a defect, but the ISTQB glossary lists "bug" as a synonym of "defect". Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

Errors and defects are not the only cause of failures. Failures can also be caused by environmental conditions, such as hardware or low quality elements.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

ISTQB describes a chain of events. A human makes an error, the error produces a defect, and the defect may cause a failure <Cite n={1} />.

| Term | Meaning in ISTQB | Example (the tutorial's own) |
|---|---|---|
| Error | The glossary says "A human action that results in a defect." Another word for it is "mistake". <Cite n={2} /> | A developer misreads the transfer limit as 10,000, not 1,000. |
| Defect | An imperfection in a work product. The work product does not meet its requirements, or the flaw impairs its intended use. <Cite n={3} /> | The code checks the limit against 10,000. |
| Failure | An event in which a component or system does not meet its requirements while it runs. <Cite n={4} /> | A user transfers 5,000 and the system accepts it. |
| Root cause | A source of a defect. If you remove it, the defect type occurs less often or not at all. <Cite n={5} /> | The team has no review of changes to the transfer limit. |

**Why people make errors**

People make errors for many reasons. The syllabus gives examples: time pressure, complex work products, processes, infrastructure or interactions, tiredness, and a lack of training <Cite n={1} />.

**Where defects live**

A defect can be in a document, for example a requirements specification or a test script. It can also be in source code or in a build file <Cite n={1} />. A defect in an early work product often leads to defective work products later, if nobody finds it <Cite n={1} />.

**When a defect becomes a failure**

A defect causes a failure only when the code runs. Some defects always cause a failure when they run. Some cause a failure only in specific circumstances. Some never cause a failure <Cite n={1} />.

**Other causes of failures**

Defects are not the only reason for failures. The environment can also cause them. For example, radiation or electromagnetic fields can cause defects in firmware <Cite n={1} />.

**Root cause analysis**

Teams usually do root cause analysis when a failure happens or when they find a defect. They expect that removing the root cause prevents similar defects or makes them less frequent <Cite n={1} />.

**Is a bug a defect?**

Yes. The glossary lists "bug" as a synonym of "defect", together with "fault" and "flaw" <Cite n={3} />. The syllabus uses the same words: "defects (faults, bugs)" <Cite n={1} />. ISTQB does not make a separate term of "bug".

<Callout type="tip" title="The tutorial's own advice">

Many teams say "bug" in daily talk and "defect" in formal reports. Both words mean the same thing in ISTQB terms. Pick one word for a project and use it every time. This is the tutorial's own advice, not an ISTQB rule.

</Callout>

**Testing and debugging**

Testing and debugging are two different activities. Testing finds failures or defects. Debugging finds the cause of a failure, analyzes it, and removes it <Cite n={1} />. The typical steps are to reproduce the failure, find the defect, and fix it <Cite n={1} />.

</Version>

</Technical>

Error আর defect-ই failure-এর একমাত্র কারণ নয়। Hardware বা নিম্নমানের উপাদানের মতো পরিবেশগত অবস্থাও failure ঘটাতে পারে।

ISTQB glossary failure-কে এমন একটি event বলে, যেখানে execution-এর সময় component বা system তার requirement পূরণ করে না। আর glossary "bug"-কে "defect"-এর synonym বলে। ISTQB definition দেখতে page-এর উপরে ISTQB version-এ যান।

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

ISTQB ঘটনার একটি শৃঙ্খল বর্ণনা করে। একজন মানুষ error করে, error থেকে defect তৈরি হয়, আর defect থেকে failure হতে পারে <Cite n={1} />।

- **Error**: এমন একটি মানুষের কাজ, যার ফলে defect হয়। এর আরেক নাম "mistake" <Cite n={2} />।
- **Defect**: কোনো work product-এর একটি খুঁত। Work product তার requirement পূরণ করে না, অথবা খুঁতটি তার উদ্দেশ্যমূলক ব্যবহারে বাধা দেয় <Cite n={3} />।
- **Failure**: এমন একটি event, যেখানে চলার সময় একটি component বা system তার requirement পূরণ করে না <Cite n={4} />।
- **Root cause**: Defect-এর একটি উৎস। এটি সরালে সেই ধরনের defect কম হয় বা আর হয় না <Cite n={5} />।

Defect তখনই failure ঘটায়, যখন code চলে। কিছু defect চললে সবসময় failure ঘটায়, কিছু শুধু নির্দিষ্ট পরিস্থিতিতে, আর কিছু কখনোই না <Cite n={1} />। পরিবেশও failure ঘটাতে পারে; যেমন radiation বা electromagnetic field firmware-এ defect তৈরি করতে পারে <Cite n={1} />।

ISTQB-তে "bug" হলো "defect"-এর synonym, "fault" আর "flaw"-এর মতোই <Cite n={3} />। Testing failure বা defect খুঁজে বের করে। Debugging failure-এর কারণ খুঁজে বের করে, বিশ্লেষণ করে, এবং দূর করে <Cite n={1} />।

## Testing Shows the Presence of Defects

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

**1. Testing shows presence of defects, not their absence**

**Meaning:** Testing can find bugs, but it cannot prove the system is 100% bug-free.

**Example:** Even after full QA of a payment system, a rare edge-case transaction failure may still appear in production due to an untested network condition.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Testing can show that a test object contains defects. Testing cannot prove that the test object has none <Cite n={1} />.

Testing lowers the probability that defects stay undiscovered. But if testing finds no defects, that is still not proof that the test object is correct <Cite n={1} />.

| Testing can | Testing cannot |
|---|---|
| Show that defects are present | Prove that no defects remain |
| Reduce the chance of hidden defects | Prove that the test object is correct |

<Callout type="tip" title="The tutorial's own advice">

Report a clean test run with care. Write "these tests found no defects", not "the product has no defects". This is the tutorial's own advice, not an ISTQB rule.

</Callout>

</Version>

</Technical>
<Layman>

<Version of="notes">

**Principle 1: Testing shows that defects are present, not that they are absent**

Testing can find bugs. It cannot prove that the system is 100% bug-free.

Example: A team does full QA of a payment system. A rare edge-case transaction can still fail in production, because of a network condition that nobody tested.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Testing can show that a test object has defects. Testing cannot prove that the test object has none <Cite n={1} />.

Testing lowers the chance that defects stay hidden. But if testing finds no defects, that does not prove that the test object is correct <Cite n={1} />.

The tutorial's own advice, not an ISTQB rule: report a clean test run with care. Write "these tests found no defects", not "the product has no defects".

</Version>

</Layman>
<Bangla>

<Version of="notes">

**1. Testing defect থাকার প্রমাণ দেখায়, না থাকার নয়**

Testing bug খুঁজে পেতে পারে, কিন্তু system যে 100% bug-free, তা প্রমাণ করতে পারে না।

উদাহরণ: একটি payment system-এর পূর্ণ QA-এর পরেও, test না করা কোনো network condition-এর কারণে production-এ একটি বিরল edge-case transaction fail করতে পারে।

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Testing দেখাতে পারে যে একটি test object-এ defect আছে। কিন্তু testing প্রমাণ করতে পারে না যে test object-এ কোনো defect নেই <Cite n={1} />।

Testing defect লুকিয়ে থাকার সম্ভাবনা কমায়। তবে testing কোনো defect না পেলেও, সেটা test object সঠিক হওয়ার প্রমাণ নয় <Cite n={1} />।

Tutorial-এর নিজের পরামর্শ (ISTQB-এর নিয়ম নয়): defect ছাড়া শেষ হওয়া test run সাবধানে report করুন। লিখুন "এই test-গুলো কোনো defect পায়নি", "product-এ কোনো defect নেই" নয়।

</Version>

</Bangla>

**2. Exhaustive testing is impossible**

**Meaning:** You cannot test all inputs, combinations, and scenarios.

**Example:** A login form with multiple browsers, devices, password combinations, and network conditions makes it impossible to test every single possibility.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Except in trivial cases, a team cannot test everything <Cite n={1} />.

## Early Testing Saves Time and Money

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

**3. Early testing saves cost and time**

**Meaning:** Defects found earlier are cheaper and easier to fix.

**Example:** A requirement mistake found during design review costs minutes to fix, but the same bug found after release may require major code rewrite and deployment rollback.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

A defect that you remove early does not cause later defects in the work products that come from it. Fewer failures happen late in the software development life cycle, so the cost of quality goes down <Cite n={1} />.

To find defects early, start static testing and dynamic testing as early as possible <Cite n={1} />. The glossary calls this approach "shift left": testing and quality assurance activities happen as early as possible in the life cycle <Cite n={6} />.

See [Test Types](/fundamentals/test-types/) for static and dynamic testing.

</Version>

</Technical>
<Layman>

<Version of="notes">

**Principle 3: Early testing saves cost and time**

A defect that you find early costs less and is easier to fix.

Example: A design review finds a mistake in a requirement. The fix takes minutes. If the same bug is found after release, it can need a major code rewrite and a deployment rollback.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

If you remove a defect early, it does not cause more defects in the work products that come from it. Fewer failures happen late in the software development life cycle, so the cost of quality goes down <Cite n={1} />.

To find defects early, start static testing and dynamic testing as early as possible <Cite n={1} />. The glossary calls this approach "shift left": testing and quality assurance activities happen as early as possible in the life cycle <Cite n={6} />.

See [Test Types](/fundamentals/test-types/) for static and dynamic testing.

</Version>

</Layman>
<Bangla>

<Version of="notes">

**3. আগে testing করলে খরচ আর সময় বাঁচে**

যত আগে defect পাওয়া যায়, তা ঠিক করা তত সস্তা ও সহজ।

উদাহরণ: design review-এর সময় পাওয়া requirement-এর একটি ভুল ঠিক করতে কয়েক মিনিট লাগে। কিন্তু একই bug release-এর পরে পাওয়া গেলে বড় code rewrite আর deployment rollback লাগতে পারে।

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

আগে সরিয়ে ফেলা একটি defect, তার থেকে তৈরি পরের work product-গুলোতে নতুন defect ঘটায় না। Software development life cycle-এর শেষ দিকে কম failure হয়, তাই cost of quality কমে <Cite n={1} />।

আগে defect খুঁজতে static testing আর dynamic testing যত আগে সম্ভব শুরু করুন <Cite n={1} />। Glossary এই পদ্ধতিকে "shift left" বলে: testing আর quality assurance-এর কাজ life cycle-এ যত আগে সম্ভব হয় <Cite n={6} />।

Static আর dynamic testing-এর জন্য দেখুন [Test Types](/fundamentals/test-types/)।

</Version>

## Defects Cluster Together

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

**4. Defects cluster together (Pareto principle)**

**Meaning:** Most bugs come from a small number of modules.

**Example:** In an e-commerce app, 70% of bugs come from payment and checkout modules, while static pages have very few issues.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Usually, a few components of the system hold most of the defects that testing finds. Or a few components cause most of the failures in operation <Cite n={1} />.

The syllabus calls this an illustration of the Pareto principle <Cite n={1} />.

## Tests Wear Out

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

**5. Tests wear out (Pesticide paradox)**

**Meaning:** Repeating the same tests will stop finding new bugs over time.

**Example:** Running the same regression suite every release may miss new edge cases in a newly added discount feature.

<Callout type="warning">

The notes name this principle "Pesticide paradox", but the ISTQB v4.0.1 syllabus names it "Tests wear out" and does not use the other name. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

If a team repeats the same tests many times, the tests find fewer and fewer new defects <Cite n={1} />.

To fight this effect, the team may need to change the existing tests and test data. It may also need to write new tests <Cite n={1} />.

Repeating tests is not always bad. In automated regression testing, it can be useful to repeat the same tests <Cite n={1} />. Regression testing detects defects that a change introduced or uncovered in unchanged areas of the software <Cite n={7} />.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The tests find no new defects in a changing area | Change the tests and the test data, and write new tests |
| You want to check whether a change added defects in unchanged areas | Repeat the automated regression tests <Cite n={7} /> |

The term "pesticide paradox" is a common name for this principle. The ISTQB syllabus and glossary do not define this term.

<Callout type="note" title="The tutorial's own explanation">

The name comes from farming. Pests become resistant when you use the same pesticide again and again. In the same way, defects survive when you run the same tests again and again. The syllabus uses the name "Tests wear out" instead.

</Callout>

</Version>

</Technical>

## Absence-of-Defects Fallacy

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Version of="notes">

**7. Absence-of-errors fallacy**

**Meaning:** A bug-free system is still useless if it does not meet user needs.

**Example:** A travel app with zero bugs is still a failure if it does not show correct flight prices or lacks essential booking features users expect.

<Callout type="warning">

The notes name this principle "Absence-of-errors fallacy", but the ISTQB v4.0.1 syllabus names it "Absence-of-defects fallacy". Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

A fallacy is a misconception. The misconception here is that verification alone ensures the success of a system <Cite n={1} />.

A team can test all the specified requirements and fix every defect it finds. The system can still fail the users. It may not meet their needs, it may not help the business goals, or it may be worse than competing systems <Cite n={1} />.

The remedy is to do validation as well as verification <Cite n={1} />.

| Activity | Question | Glossary meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Verification | Does the work product meet its specification? | Checking that a work product meets its specification <Cite n={8} /> |
| Validation | Does the work product match what stakeholders need? | Checking by examination that a work product fits what a stakeholder needs <Cite n={9} /> |

The term "absence-of-errors fallacy" is a common name for this principle. The ISTQB syllabus and glossary do not define this term.

<Callout type="note" title="The tutorial's own explanation">

The syllabus names this principle "Absence-of-defects fallacy". In ISTQB terms, an error is a human action. A defect is the flaw that the error produces. So the syllabus name is the more exact one. The idea is the same: a product with no defects can still be the wrong product.

</Callout>
