# Testing Basics

What testing is, why it matters, and the skills a tester needs.

<PageMeta />

## What Is Software Testing

<Version of="notes">

**Software Testing** is the process of evaluating a software application to find defects, verify requirements, and ensure it works as expected before release. It involves executing the system (manually or via automation) and checking whether actual behavior matches expected behavior.

You can think of it as: *"checking software quality by validating and breaking it before users do."*

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB says testing is more than executing the system, because static testing finds defects without running the software. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB says testing can show that defects are present but cannot prove that the software is correct, so testing cannot ensure that it works as expected. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Software testing is a set of activities. <Cite n={1} /> The activities find defects and check the quality of software work products. <Cite n={1} /> A work product under test is a test object. <Cite n={1} />

The glossary places testing inside the software development lifecycle (SDLC). Testing evaluates the quality of a component or system and of the related work products. <Cite n={2} />

Testing is more than running tests. It also includes other activities, and it must fit the SDLC. <Cite n={1} />

Testing can show that defects are present. It cannot prove that the test object is correct, also when it finds no defects. <Cite n={1} />

Testing involves two checks. <Cite n={1} />

| Check | Question | Glossary meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Verification | Does the system meet the specified requirements? <Cite n={1} /> | A work product fulfills its specification. <Cite n={3} /> |
| Validation | Does the system meet the needs of users and other stakeholders in its operational environment? <Cite n={1} /> | A work product matches the needs of a stakeholder. <Cite n={4} /> |

Testing is dynamic or static. Dynamic testing runs the software. Static testing does not run it. Static testing includes reviews and static analysis. <Cite n={1} />

Testing is largely an intellectual activity. It needs specialized knowledge, analytical skills, critical thinking, and systems thinking. Testers use tools, but the tools do not change this. <Cite n={1} />

**Testing and debugging**

Testing and debugging are separate activities. <Cite n={1} /> Dynamic testing can trigger a failure. Static testing can find a defect directly in the test object. <Cite n={1} /> The glossary says debugging finds, analyzes, and removes the causes of failures. <Cite n={5} />

After dynamic testing triggers a failure, the typical debugging process has three steps. <Cite n={1} />

1. Reproduce the failure.
2. Diagnose it: find the defect.
3. Fix the defect.

Confirmation testing then checks that the fix solved the problem. Regression testing checks that the fix did not cause failures elsewhere in the test object. <Cite n={1} />

When static testing finds a defect, debugging only removes it. Nobody needs to reproduce or diagnose a failure, because static testing causes no failure. <Cite n={1} />

</Version>

## Why Testing Is Necessary

<Version of="notes">

- **Finds defects early** → reduces cost of fixing bugs after release
- **Ensures requirements are met** → builds the right product, not just a working one
- **Improves product quality** → stability, performance, usability
- **Prevents business loss** → avoids failures in production (money, reputation, users)
- **Enhances security** → detects vulnerabilities before attackers do
- **Builds user trust** → reliable software increases adoption

> *In short: without testing, software is a "guess"; with testing, it becomes a "reliable product."*

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB separates verification (the system meets the specified requirements) from validation (the system meets the needs of users and stakeholders), so meeting requirements alone does not show that it is the right product. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

Software that does not work can cost money, time, or business reputation. In extreme cases it can cause injury or death. <Cite n={1} /> Testing reduces the risk of failure in operation. <Cite n={1} />

Testing makes these contributions to a project.

| Contribution | What testing does |
|---|---|
| Finds defects at a low cost | Testing is a cost-effective way to detect defects. Debugging removes them, so testing improves quality only indirectly. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Measures quality directly | At various phases of the SDLC, testing evaluates how good a test object is. Managers use the result for decisions, such as the release decision. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Represents the users | Testers keep the needs of users in view during development. A project can rarely involve enough real users, because of cost and availability. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Meets obligations | A contract, a law, or a regulatory standard can require testing. <Cite n={1} /> |

The contribution of testing is not limited to the test team. Any stakeholder can apply testing skills to help the project succeed. <Cite n={1} />

<Callout type="tip" title="The tutorial's own advice">

The "My notes" list gives business reasons: money, reputation, and trust. The ISTQB table gives the contributions that testing makes inside a project. Use both when you explain the value of testing to a manager.

</Callout>

</Version>

## Quality and Quality Assurance

<Version of="notes">

- **Quality**: A degree of excellence of a product or a service.
- **Quality assurance (QA)**: is any systematic process of determining whether a product or service meets specified requirements.

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB defines quality as the degree to which a work product satisfies stated and implied requirements, not as a degree of excellence. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB defines QA as activities that give confidence that quality requirements will be fulfilled, and it says that testing and QA are not the same. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

The glossary defines quality as the degree to which a work product satisfies stated and implied requirements. <Cite n={6} />

The glossary defines quality assurance (QA) as activities that give confidence that quality requirements will be fulfilled. <Cite n={7} />

The syllabus adds more detail about QA. <Cite n={1} />

- QA is process-oriented and preventive. It focuses on the implementation and improvement of processes.
- QA starts from one idea: a good process, followed correctly, generates a good product.
- QA applies to the development process and to the test process.
- QA is the responsibility of everyone on the project.

People often use "testing" and "QA" as the same word, but they are not the same. <Cite n={1} /> Both use test results. Testing uses them to fix defects. QA uses them as feedback on the performance of the development and test processes. <Cite n={1} />

</Version>

## Testing Objectives

<Version of="notes">

**1. Functional Correctness**

Checks whether the software works according to requirements and business rules.

- Valid inputs produce correct outputs
- Logic and calculations are accurate
- Handles edge cases properly

**2. Authorization**

Ensures users can only access permitted features and data.

- Role-based access control works correctly
- Protected APIs cannot be accessed without permission
- Unauthorized actions are blocked

**3. Service Level (Performance & Reliability)**

Measures how well the system performs under real usage.

- Fast response time
- Handles expected load
- Stable under stress
- High availability

**4. Usability**

Evaluates how easy the system is to use for real users.

- Simple and clear UI
- Smooth navigation
- Clear error messages
- User-friendly workflow

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

The syllabus lists nine typical test objectives. <Cite n={1} />

- Evaluate work products, for example the requirements, user stories, designs, and code.
- Cause failures and find defects.
- Reach the required coverage of a test object.
- Reduce the risk of inadequate software quality.
- Verify that the specified requirements are met.
- Verify that the test object follows contractual, legal, and regulatory requirements.
- Give stakeholders the information they need to make informed decisions.
- Build confidence in the quality of the test object.
- Validate that the test object is complete and works as the stakeholders expect.

Finding defects is only one of the nine objectives. <Cite n={1} />

The objectives change with the context. The context includes the work product, the test level, the risks, the SDLC, and the business. Business factors are, for example, the structure of the company, competition, and time to market. <Cite n={1} />

Functional testing checks what the test object does. Non-functional testing checks how well it behaves. <Cite n={1} /> The syllabus uses the ISO/IEC 25010 list of non-functional quality characteristics. <Cite n={1} />

| Quality characteristic |
|---|
| Performance efficiency |
| Compatibility |
| Usability |
| Reliability |
| Security |
| Maintainability |
| Portability |
| Safety |

<Callout type="note" title="The tutorial's own explanation">

The four objectives of the "My notes" version fit this list. Functional correctness is the work of functional testing. Authorization belongs to security. Service level covers performance efficiency and reliability. Usability keeps its name. This mapping is the tutorial's own, not ISTQB text.

</Callout>

</Version>

## Software Testing Misconceptions

<Version of="notes">

**1. Without knowing programming, anyone can do software testing**

**Fact:** Basic UI manual testing can be done using common sense and domain understanding, but real testing goes far beyond that. Whitebox testing, API testing, client-server validation, data testing, and test automation require strong programming and database knowledge to ensure correct verification of system behavior.

**2. QA is getting replaced by AI**

**Fact:** AI is reducing repetitive manual testing tasks, but testing itself is not disappearing. Manual QA roles are shrinking, while demand for AI-assisted SDET and technical QA engineers is increasing. The focus is shifting toward engineers who can design, automate, and validate quality using AI tools.

**3. Testing is only about finding bugs**

**Fact:** Bug detection is an important objective, but not the only one. The main goal of testing is ensuring overall software quality. This requires technical understanding to validate functional and non-functional aspects such as performance, security, usability, and reliability of production-grade systems.

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB names no single main goal of testing, because the objectives depend on the context. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

**4. Automation testing tools are enough, programming is not required**

**Fact:** Automation tools only provide the framework; real value comes from coding ability. Test scripts, framework design, assertions, debugging failures, and maintaining test suites all require solid programming skills.

**5. QA is an easy switch career for fresh graduates**

**Fact:** While entry into QA may seem easier, long-term growth requires continuous technical skill development. Without programming, API knowledge, and system understanding, career progression in QA becomes limited.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

The syllabus names two common misconceptions about testing. <Cite n={1} />

| Misconception | What ISTQB says |
|---|---|
| Testing only means running tests. | Testing also includes other activities, and it must fit the SDLC. Static testing, for example, does not run the software. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Testing only verifies the test object. | Testing also validates. It checks that the system meets the needs of users and other stakeholders. <Cite n={1} /> |

The syllabus makes three more points that help against wrong ideas about testing.

- Testing is not only a technical activity. Teams must also plan, manage, estimate, monitor, and control it. <Cite n={1} />
- Testing cannot prove that no defects exist. This stays true when testing finds no defects. <Cite n={1} />
- Finding defects is one of nine test objectives. See the topic "Testing Objectives". <Cite n={1} />

The ISTQB syllabus and glossary do not cover four of the five claims of the "My notes" version. These four claims are about programming, AI, automation tools, and a career in QA. The nine test objectives answer the third claim, "Testing is only about finding bugs".

<Callout type="note" title="The tutorial's own explanation">

The four claims are the views of the owner of this tutorial about the work of a tester. They are not ISTQB statements. The topic "QA Generic Skills" shows the skills that the syllabus names.

</Callout>

</Version>

## QA Generic Skills

<Version of="notes">

The following skills are particularly relevant for testers:

**1. Testing Knowledge**

Understanding testing concepts, test levels, test types, and test techniques to test software effectively.

**2. Attention to Detail**

Being careful, thorough, curious, and methodical to find defects that others may miss.

**3. Communication Skills**

Explaining issues clearly, listening actively, working with the team, and reporting defects in an understandable way.

**4. Analytical & Critical Thinking**

Analyzing requirements, questioning unclear logic, identifying risks, and thinking creatively to find hidden problems.

**5. Technical Knowledge**

Using tools, APIs, databases, logs, automation, and other technical skills to test more efficiently.

**6. Domain Knowledge**

Understanding the business domain so testers can validate whether the software solves real user needs correctly.

**Simple Summary**

A good QA is not just a bug finder. A good QA needs **testing knowledge, sharp observation, communication, analytical thinking, technical skill, and business understanding**.

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

A skill is the ability to do a task well. It comes from knowledge, practice, and aptitude. <Cite n={1} /> The syllabus lists six generic skills that are particularly relevant for testers. <Cite n={1} />

| Skill | Why a tester needs it |
|---|---|
| Testing knowledge | It makes testing more effective, for example when you use test techniques. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Thoroughness, carefulness, curiosity, attention to detail, being methodical | These help you find defects, especially the defects that are hard to find. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Communication, active listening, being a team player | These help you work with all stakeholders, pass on information, be understood, and report and discuss defects. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Analytical thinking, critical thinking, creativity | These make testing more effective. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Technical knowledge | It makes testing more efficient, for example when you use suitable test tools. <Cite n={1} /> |
| Domain knowledge | It helps you understand end users and business representatives, and talk with them. <Cite n={1} /> |

Testers often bring bad news. People tend to blame the person who brings it. <Cite n={1} /> A test result can feel like criticism of the product and its author. <Cite n={1} /> Confirmation bias also makes it hard to accept information that disagrees with a held belief. <Cite n={1} />

So communicate information about defects and failures in a constructive way. <Cite n={1} />

</Version>

## QA vs QC

<Version of="notes">

| **QA** | **QC** |
| --- | --- |
| QA makes sure you are doing the right things. | QC makes sure the results of what you've done are what you expected. |
| QA aims to prevent the defect. So this is a preventive approach. | QC aims to identify and improve the defects. So this is a corrective approach. |
| QA process starts before executing the program. | QC always involves after executing the program. |
| QA is a verification process | QC is a validation process |
| QA means Planning for doing a process. | QC means Action for executing the planned process. |
| QA is the process to create the deliverables. | QC is the process that validate to deliverables. |
| QA is responsible for full SDLC | QC is responsible for STLC |

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB says that testing, a major form of quality control, includes static testing that finds defects without executing the program, so QC does not always come after execution. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB does not give verification to QA and validation to QC, because it says that testing involves both. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB says that QA improves the processes that make the deliverables, and it does not make the deliverables itself. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

ISTQB says that QA is the responsibility of everyone on the project, not of one team, and it has no term STLC. Switch to the ISTQB version at the top of the page to see the ISTQB definition.

</Callout>

</Version>

<Version of="istqb">

The syllabus compares testing and QA. <Cite n={1} /> The glossary defines quality control (QC) as activities designed to evaluate the quality of a component or system. <Cite n={8} /> Testing is a major form of QC. Other forms are formal methods, simulation, and prototyping. <Cite n={1} />

| Aspect | QA | Testing (a major form of QC) |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Process-oriented <Cite n={1} /> | Product-oriented <Cite n={1} /> |
| Approach | Preventive <Cite n={1} /> | Corrective <Cite n={1} /> |
| Focus | The implementation and improvement of processes <Cite n={1} /> | Activities that support an appropriate level of quality <Cite n={1} /> |
| Scope | The development and test processes. Everyone on the project is responsible. <Cite n={1} /> | The evaluation of the quality of a component, a system, and related work products <Cite n={2} /> |
| Use of test results | Feedback on how well the processes perform <Cite n={1} /> | Fixing defects <Cite n={1} /> |

Verification and validation belong to testing. The syllabus does not split them between QA and QC. <Cite n={1} />

The ISTQB syllabus and glossary do not define the term STLC.

<Callout type="note" title="The tutorial's own explanation">

STLC means Software Testing Life Cycle. People use it for the phases of the test process. ISTQB describes the same ground as test activities. The chapter [SDLC, STLC and Agile](/fundamentals/lifecycles/) maps the two.

</Callout>
