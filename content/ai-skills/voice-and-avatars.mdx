# Voice and Avatars

AI can now speak in a natural voice and show a talking person who was never filmed. For a tester, this is a source of test data for voice features, and also a risk area: consent, disclosure, and deepfakes.

<PageMeta />

## In plain words

<Voices>
<Technical>

This skill has three parts:

- **Text to speech (TTS).** A model turns text into speech. ElevenLabs describes its TTS as lifelike audio with natural intonation and pacing <Cite n={2} />.
- **Voice cloning.** A model copies the sound of one real voice. ElevenLabs has Instant Voice Cloning, which works from short samples, and Professional Voice Cloning, which trains a more realistic model <Cite n={1} />.
- **Avatars.** A video of a person who speaks a script, made from a real person's likeness. Synthesia calls this a custom avatar: an avatar with your likeness <Cite n={4} />.

The rule that goes with all three: **always tell people that a voice or a face is AI**. The EU AI Act says that people should know when they interact with a machine, and that deep fakes must be clearly labelled <Cite n={5} />.

</Technical>

## How it works

<Voices>
<Technical>

**TTS.** You send text and pick a voice. The model returns audio. ElevenLabs returns `mp3` by default <Cite n={2} />, and it also offers PCM, Opus, and μ-law and A-law at 8 kHz, which are made for telephony <Cite n={2} />. Settings such as Stability and Similarity control how consistent the voice is <Cite n={2} />.

```text
text + voice + settings --> TTS model --> audio file (mp3, pcm, mu-law, ...)
```

**Cloning and consent.** Vendors build consent into the flow:

- ElevenLabs Professional Voice Clones need a verification step that confirms the voice belongs to you, and they can only clone your own voice <Cite n={1} />.
- HeyGen asks for a short consent video for every video-based Digital Twin, and the person in the avatar records their own consent <Cite n={3} />. They read the given consent text exactly as written <Cite n={3} />.
- Synthesia makes an avatar of a person only with that person's explicit consent <Cite n={4} />.

**Policy.** ElevenLabs says its policies forbid use for any illegal or harmful purpose <Cite n={1} />. In the EU, the AI Act adds the duty to label deep fakes <Cite n={5} />. Read [AI Governance](/ai-skills/ai-governance/) for the wider rules.

</Technical>

## How a tester uses it

<Voices>
<Technical>

- **Test voice features.** If your product has voice search, a voice bot, or speech-to-text, you need many spoken inputs. Generate them with TTS from a list of test phrases: short commands, long sentences, numbers, and names.
- **Synthetic speech test data.** Make the same phrase in several voices and with different Stability settings <Cite n={2} /> to test how robust the recognizer is. For a phone system, generate 8 kHz μ-law audio <Cite n={2} />, the format that telephony uses.
- **Test the disclosure.** If your product uses an AI voice or avatar, test that it tells the user so, for example a spoken notice at the start of a call or a visible label on a video <Cite n={5} />.
- **Test the consent flow.** If your product lets users clone a voice or make an avatar, write test cases for the consent step: no consent, the wrong person, and a script that is not read exactly <Cite n={3} />.

<Callout type="tip" title="Tutorial advice">
This is the tutorial's own advice. Use only your own voice, a stock voice, or a voice whose owner gave written consent. Keep synthetic test audio in the test data folder with a clear name, so nobody mistakes it for a real recording.
</Callout>

</Technical>

- **টেস্ট ডেটা হিসেবে কৃত্রিম কথা।** একই বাক্য কয়েকটা ভয়েসে ও ভিন্ন Stability সেটিংয়ে বানান <Cite n={2} />। এতে recognizer কতটা মজবুত তা টেস্ট হয়। ফোন সিস্টেমের জন্য 8 kHz μ-law অডিও বানান <Cite n={2} />, যে ফরম্যাট টেলিফোনি ব্যবহার করে।
- **ঘোষণাটা টেস্ট করুন।** প্রোডাক্ট AI ভয়েস বা avatar ব্যবহার করলে টেস্ট করুন যে সেটা ইউজারকে জানায়। যেমন কলের শুরুতে একটা কথ্য নোটিস, বা ভিডিওতে একটা দৃশ্যমান লেবেল <Cite n={5} />।

## Common mistakes

<Voices>
<Technical>

- **Cloning a voice without consent.** This can harm a real person, and ElevenLabs Professional Voice Clones can only clone your own voice <Cite n={1} />. Fix: clone only your own voice, or use stock voices.
- **No AI label.** Viewers may think an avatar is a real employee. Fix: label AI voices and avatars every time; the EU AI Act requires labels for deep fakes <Cite n={5} />.
- **Test audio that is too clean.** Real users speak with noise, accents, and pauses. Fix: mix synthetic audio with real recordings that people gave consent for.
- **Wrong audio format.** A phone system may reject or distort `mp3` input. Fix: generate the format that the system uses, such as μ-law at 8 kHz <Cite n={2} />.
- **Ignoring deepfake risk.** A cloned voice can be used to trick a help desk or a colleague. Fix: test that your product does not trust a voice alone for identity, and raise it as a security risk.

</Technical>
<Layman>

- **Cloning a voice without consent.** This can hurt a real person. ElevenLabs Professional Voice Clones can only copy your own voice <Cite n={1} />. Fix: clone only your own voice, or use stock voices.
- **No AI label.** Viewers may think an avatar is a real employee. Fix: label AI voices and avatars every time. The EU AI Act asks for labels on deep fakes <Cite n={5} />.
- **Test audio that is too clean.** Real users speak with noise, accents, and pauses. Fix: mix made-up audio with real recordings that people agreed to give.
- **Wrong audio format.** A phone system may reject or spoil `mp3` input. Fix: make the format that the system uses, such as μ-law at 8 kHz <Cite n={2} />.
- **Ignoring the deepfake risk.** A cloned voice can trick a help desk or a colleague. Fix: test that your product does not trust a voice alone to know who someone is. Report it as a security risk.

## How to start

<Voices>
<Technical>

1. Ask whether your company allows AI voice tools, and which voices you may use.
2. Write 20 test phrases for a voice feature of your product, with numbers, names, and edge cases.
3. Generate each phrase with a stock voice in the format that your system needs <Cite n={2} />.
4. Run the phrases through the feature and record which ones it gets wrong.
5. Add one test case that checks the AI disclosure of your product <Cite n={5} />.

</Technical>
<Layman>

1. Ask whether your company allows AI voice tools. Ask which voices you may use.
2. Write 20 test phrases for a voice feature of your product. Include numbers, names, and edge cases.
3. Make each phrase with a stock voice, in the format that your system needs <Cite n={2} />.
4. Run the phrases through the feature. Note which ones it gets wrong.
5. Add one test case that checks the AI notice of your product <Cite n={5} />.

</Layman>
