# How a Stock Exchange Works

A stock exchange is a meeting place where people who want to buy a share meet people who want to sell it. You tap "buy" in a trading app, and the exchange tries to pair your order with a seller. Behind that tap, the exchange must check your money, put your order in a fair place in line, and find the right seller. It must do this for huge numbers of orders, and it must never break the rules of fairness. This page follows one article that designs a simple exchange. The article describes one way to build it. It does not describe one named exchange.

- Trade stocks only.

- Limit how many shares one user can trade each day.
- Publish market data (prices and trades) in real time.

|---|---|

                                                                        |

<Callout type="note" title="What event-sourced means">
In an event-sourced system, the main record is a list of everything that happened, in order. The current state is what you get when you replay that list from the start. A bank statement works the same way: your balance is the sum of all the lines.
</Callout>

## Orders and the order book

<Voices>
<Technical>

Think of two queues at a busy market stall. One queue holds buyers, sorted so the highest offer stands at the front. The other holds sellers, sorted so the lowest price stands at the front.

That pair of queues is the order book. For one stock, it shows every waiting buy and sell order, with its amount and its price <Cite n={1} />. Buy orders are sorted from the highest price down. Sell orders are sorted from the lowest price up <Cite n={1} />. A trade happens where the two sides meet <Cite n={1} />.

The article explains a few trading terms <Cite n={1} />:

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Market order | "Buy or sell now at the current price." It fills at once if there are enough shares on the other side. |
| Limit order | "Buy or sell only at my price or better." A buy limit of 100 can fill at 100 or less. A sell limit of 100 can fill at 100 or more. |
| Spread | The gap from the best (highest) buy offer to the best (lowest) sell offer |

</Technical>

These two lines are the order book. For one stock, it shows each waiting buy order and sell order, with its amount and its price <Cite n={1} />. Buy orders go from the highest price down. Sell orders go from the lowest price up <Cite n={1} />. A trade happens where the two sides meet <Cite n={1} />.

- **Limit order.** Buy or sell only at your price or better. A buy limit of 100 can fill at 100 or less. A sell limit of 100 can fill at 100 or more.

## The broker and the FIX protocol

<Voices>
<Technical>

A broker is like a travel agent. You do not phone each airline. You ask the agent, and the agent talks to the airlines in their own language.

A user connects to the broker with REST APIs, and with WebSockets for live prices <Cite n={1} />. A WebSocket is a connection that stays open, so the server can push updates without being asked. The broker then talks to the exchange gateway in the Financial Information Exchange protocol, or FIX <Cite n={1} />.

FIX is a standard message format for trading. The article says FIX gives each order message a unique sequence number <Cite n={1} />. It also adds a checksum and the message length, so the receiver can see if a message got damaged on the way <Cite n={1} />. A checksum is a small number computed from the message. If the number does not match on arrival, the message is broken.

</Technical>
<Layman>

A broker is like a travel agent. You do not phone each airline. You ask the agent, and the agent talks to the airlines in their own language.

A user connects to the broker with REST APIs. For live prices, the user connects with WebSockets <Cite n={1} />. A WebSocket connection stays open, so the server can send updates without a request. The broker then talks to the gateway of the exchange in the Financial Information Exchange protocol (FIX) <Cite n={1} />.

FIX is a standard message format for trading. The article says that FIX gives each order message a unique sequence number <Cite n={1} />. FIX also adds a checksum and the message length, so the receiver can see if a message got damaged on the way <Cite n={1} />. A checksum is a small number that is calculated from the message. If the number is different on arrival, the message is broken.

</Layman>
<Bangla>

Broker অনেকটা travel agent-এর মতো। আপনি প্রতিটি airline-কে ফোন করেন না। আপনি agent-কে বলেন, আর agent airline-গুলোর সঙ্গে তাদের নিজের ভাষায় কথা বলে।

User REST API দিয়ে broker-এর সঙ্গে যুক্ত হন, আর live দামের জন্য WebSocket ব্যবহার করেন <Cite n={1} />। WebSocket হলো এমন একটি connection যা খোলা থাকে, তাই server না চাইতেই update পাঠাতে পারে। তারপর broker exchange gateway-এর সঙ্গে Financial Information Exchange protocol, অর্থাৎ FIX-এ কথা বলে <Cite n={1} />।

FIX হলো trading-এর জন্য একটি standard message format। Article বলে, FIX প্রতিটি order message-কে একটি unique sequence number দেয় <Cite n={1} />। এটি একটি checksum আর message-এর length-ও যোগ করে, যাতে পথে কোনো message নষ্ট হলে receiver তা বুঝতে পারে <Cite n={1} />। Checksum হলো message থেকে হিসাব করা একটি ছোট সংখ্যা। পৌঁছানোর পর সংখ্যাটি না মিললে message-টি ভাঙা।

## The gateway: checks before the line

<Voices>

## Sequence numbers: a ticket for every order

<Voices>
<Technical>

At a bakery, you pull a paper ticket when you walk in. The baker serves ticket 41 before ticket 42, no matter who shouts louder. Nobody can argue about who came first.

The order manager does the same. It gives every order, cancel, and fill a number that always goes up across the whole exchange <Cite n={1} />. A fill is a completed trade, in full or in part. The article lists what these numbers give <Cite n={1} />:

- **Fair order.** Orders are handled in the order they arrived.
- **Fast recovery.** After a crash, the system can replay events in the same order and reach the same state.
- **Exactly once.** Each event takes effect one time, not zero times and not twice.
- **Gap detection.** A missing number shows at once that an event got lost <Cite n={1} />.

The article adds one more idea. Each message type (new order, cancel, trade) gets its own topic queue with its own numbers <Cite n={1} />. A topic queue is a named line of messages that a program reads in order. Separate lines keep each stream in order, let each type move at its own pace, and make replay simpler <Cite n={1} />.

</Technical>
<Layman>

At a bakery, you take a ticket when you come in. The baker serves ticket 41 before ticket 42, even if another person shouts louder.

The order manager gives each order, cancel, and fill a number that always goes up across the whole exchange <Cite n={1} />. A fill is a completed trade, full or partial. These numbers give <Cite n={1} />:

- **Fair order.** The system handles orders in the order that they arrived.
- **Fast recovery.** After a crash, the system can replay the events in the same order and get the same state.
- **Exactly once.** Each event has an effect one time. Not zero times, and not two times.
- **Gap detection.** A missing number shows immediately that an event is lost <Cite n={1} />.

Each message type (new order, cancel, trade) also gets its own topic queue with its own numbers <Cite n={1} />. A topic queue is a named line of messages that a program reads in order. Separate lines keep each stream in order, let each type move at its own speed, and make replay simpler <Cite n={1} />.

</Layman>

## The matching engine

<Voices>
<Technical>

The matching engine is the heart of the exchange. It checks the sequence numbers, pairs buy and sell orders, and turns trades into market data <Cite n={1} />.

**The order book in memory.** The engine keeps the order book in memory, not on disk, for each stock <Cite n={1} />. Each stock has one list for buys and one for sells <Cite n={1} />. Inside each price level, orders wait in arrival order, first in and first out <Cite n={1} />. The article uses a doubly linked list for each price level. A doubly linked list is a chain where each item knows its neighbour on both sides. Adding to the end takes constant time, and so does removing a known item <Cite n={1} />.

**Fast cancels.** Searching every price level for one order would be slow. So the engine keeps an index from order ID to the order in memory, which makes a cancel a single lookup <Cite n={1} />. This matters because cancels are about 40% of all messages.

**The matching rule.** The engine makes a trade when the buy price is equal to or higher than the sell price <Cite n={1} />. The engine also tracks the best buy and best sell prices so it can match quickly <Cite n={1} />. A closed order leaves the book and goes into the trade history <Cite n={1} />.

<Callout type="note" title="Why the engine must be deterministic">
The article says the same input sequence must always produce the same output sequence <Cite n={1} />. "Deterministic" means no luck and no timing effects. If a replay after a crash gave different trades, the exchange could not prove what really happened.
</Callout>

The free part of the article stops after the matching logic. This page covers only that free part.

</Technical>

## Key words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Order | A request to buy or sell a number of shares, maybe at a set price |
| Order book | The two sorted lists of waiting buy and sell orders for one stock |
| Matching engine | The program that pairs buy and sell orders into trades |
| Broker | The app or firm that sends your orders to the exchange |
| Gateway | The front door that checks orders before they reach the engine |
| Sequence number | A number that always goes up and fixes the order of events |
| Queue | A line of messages read in order |
| Market data | The live stream of prices and trades that the exchange publishes |
| Event sourcing | Storing every change as an event, so you can replay history |
| Latency | The delay between sending a message and getting the result |

## What a tester can learn

<Voices>
<Technical>

<Callout type="tip" title="Tutorial advice">
These test ideas are the tutorial's own advice, not claims from the article.

- **Price and time priority.** Place two buy orders at the same price a moment apart. Check that the earlier one fills first.
- **Replay gives the same result.** Feed a recorded order stream into the engine twice. Check that the two trade outputs are byte for byte the same.
- **Cancel races.** Send a cancel at the same moment as a matching order. Check that the order either fills or cancels, never both.
- **Gaps and duplicates.** Drop or repeat one sequence number on purpose. Check that the system detects the gap and does not apply a trade twice.
- **Limits and funds.** Try to trade more than the daily limit, or more than the wallet holds. Check that the risk manager rejects the order.
- **Cancel storms.** Load test with about 40% cancels, not only new orders, to match the real message mix.
</Callout>

</Technical>
